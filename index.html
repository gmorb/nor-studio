    CH341A programmer

    ⚡
    Needs the 3.3 V mod. Unmodified black CH341A boards put ~5 V on the signal pins and can kill the NOR chip or the PS5. 1.8 V chips also need a 1.8 V adapter on top of that.

    Read the NOR straight into the editor, then write your patched dump back. Only the 4 KB sectors that changed are rewritten, and everything is verified.

    Wiring check

    Tests each CH341A connection one at a time, plus the chip's HOLD and WP pins. You'll need a multimeter. Keep the console unplugged. Measure on the chip's own legs, not on the clip.

    Read

    Dumps the whole chip. Reading twice and comparing catches a loose clip before you trust the dump.

    Write

    Load or read a dump first. If you've made edits in the NOR Editor, the patched version is what gets written.

      Hardware guide

      Where the NOR and UART points are, how to wire them, and whether you need to remove the chip. EDM-010/020 details come from Sony's board service manuals (CFI-1000/1100 series). Always confirm with a multimeter before connecting anything.

      NOR chip IC5001

      Winbond W25Q16JVSNIM (marked 25Q16JVNIM): 2 MB, 3.3 V, SOIC-8, chip ID EF 40 15. Top side (Side A), right of the southbridge (IC5006), with a column of 4 small parts between them. The same chip is used on EDM-010 and EDM-020.

      CH341A pin → chip pin · 3.3V* only with the board unplugged · — leave unconnected · full wiring guide
      On the board: VCC is the 3.3 V standby rail (3.3V_SS_PG1), shared with the southbridge. CS, WP and HOLD have 4.7k pull-ups (WP R5077, HOLD R5078). DO and DI go to the southbridge through 47 Ω resistors (R5058, R5060).
      Southbridge marking: SIE CXD90061GG on EDM-010 (EDM-041 uses CXD90069GG). When replacing a southbridge, fit the same part number as the original: the NOR has to match the southbridge that's fitted.

      UART test pads JL5101–JL5110

      Bottom side of the board, just below a metal shield can. A small black ribbon connector sits on the left and a white 3-pin connector on the right. The layout below is as you see it on the board, and it matches the service manual rotated 180°.

      PS5 pad (right-hand column)SignalAdapter pin
      JL5109 · topGNDGND
      JL5105 · middleSSB_UART0_TX (console talks)RX
      JL5104 · bottomSSB_UART0_RX (console listens)TX
      JL51013.3 V standby, right next to GND: measure onlydo not connect
      The right-hand column is GND / TX / RX from top to bottom. That matches the reference photo in Console Service Tool. The schematic shows no net on JL5109, so confirm it has continuity to ground before you use it. "FC" pads belong to the SSD controller.

      1 · Read error codes (UART)

      1. Adapter on 3.3 V (FT232R jumper to 3.3 V; CH341A needs the 3.3 V mod).
      2. Find the pads: with AC plugged in and the console off, JL5101 should read ≈3.3 V to ground, and JL5105 (TX) ≈3.3 V idle.
      3. Unplug AC. Solder thin wires: JL5105→RX, JL5104→TX, JL5109 (or any ground)→GND. Never connect VCC or JL5101.
      4. Plug AC back in (standby only) → UART tab → Connect → Read error codes.
      5. No reply? Swap TX/RX first, then check fuse F7003 and the NOR.

      2 · Dump / flash in-circuit

      1. AC unplugged, battery-backed rails discharged (wait a minute).
      2. 3.3 V-modded CH341A, SOIC-8 clip on IC5001, pin 1 to pin 1. No 1.8 V adapter for this chip.
      3. The clip also powers the 3.3V_SS rail that feeds the southbridge. If reads are unstable, hold JL5110 (/SSB_RESET) to GND with a wire while reading/writing so the southbridge stays off the SPI bus.
      4. Programmer tab → Connect → expect EF 40 15 (Winbond W25Q16JV). Read with "read twice" on.
      5. Save the backup, edit, then Write. It rewrites only changed sectors and verifies.

      3 · If in-circuit won't work

      1. Symptoms: ID reads 00 00 00/FF FF FF, or the two reads don't match even after reseating the clip and grounding JL5110.
      2. Lift pin 8 (VCC) only and power the chip from the clip. This isolates the rest of the rail.
      3. Or chip-off: hot air, put the chip in an SOP8 socket/adapter, read and write, then solder it back in the same orientation.
      4. Chip-off is the most reliable method and the one most repair guides use. In-circuit is quicker when it works.

      NOR on EDM-03x

      1. No EDM-03x schematic is included here. Look for the 8-pin flash chip next to the southbridge, as on the launch boards.
      2. Read its marking before clipping on. 25Q16JV means 3.3 V (CH341A with the 3.3 V mod). 25U…/…W means 1.8 V (add a 1.8 V adapter).

      Wiring a CH341A straight to the chip

      Tip: the NOR diagrams in each board tab show these CH341A labels right on the chip's pins. For when you're not using the SOIC-8 clip or the SOP8 socket adapter: wires soldered to the NOR's legs, a chip-off on a breakout, or a clip that won't fit. Applies to every PS5 board covered here, since they all use the same Winbond W25Q16JV.

      CH341A labelFlash pinWhy
      CS1 /CSChip select. Same name both ends.
      MISO2 DO / SOData from the chip out to the programmer in.
      MOSI5 DI / SIData from the programmer out to the chip in.
      CLK6 CLKClock. Also printed SCK or SCLK.
      GND4 GNDConnect this first.
      VCC 3.3 V8 VCCOnly when the board is unplugged. On a powered board, leave this wire off (see below).

      Why the names don't match

      1. SPI is named from the programmer's side. MISO means "master in, slave out", so it connects to the chip's output, DO. MOSI means "master out, slave in", so it connects to the chip's input, DI.
      2. Don't cross them like UART. In UART, TX goes to RX. In SPI, the names already account for direction: MISO→DO and MOSI→DI, never MOSI→DO.
      3. Other names you'll see: DO = SO = IO1 = Q · DI = SI = IO0 = D · CS = CE# = SS · CLK = SCK = SCLK. Some programmer boards print DO/DI instead of MISO/MOSI, and vendors disagree on whether that's the programmer's own data out or the flash pin it goes to. Wire it one way and check the chip ID; if it reads FF FF FF or 00 00 00, swap those two wires and try again.
      4. /WP (pin 3) and /HOLD (pin 7): on the board they're already pulled up to 3.3 V, so leave them alone in-circuit. On a chip-off with loose wires, tie both to VCC (pin 8), or writes fail and reads can stall.

      Before you power anything

      • ⚡
        CH341A with the 3.3 V modStock black boards put ~5 V on CS/MISO/MOSI/CLK. Measure each signal pin to GND first: ≈3.3 V is OK.
      • !
        Pick one power source, never bothUnplugged board: the programmer's 3.3 V powers the chip. Powered board: the console powers it and the 3.3 V wire stays off. See "Two ways to power the chip" below.
      • !
        Short, equal wiresKeep them under ~15 cm and roughly the same length; twist GND alongside CLK if you can. Long or loose wires are the usual cause of "two reads don't match".
      • !
        Find pin 1 by the dotPins 1–4 run along the dot side, 5–8 come back along the other side (pin 8 is opposite pin 1).

      Check your wiring with the app

      1. Programmer tab → Connect. A correct hookup shows EF 40 15 · Winbond W25Q16JV, 3.3 V.
      2. FF FF FF: MISO isn't reaching DO, or CS isn't connected. Check MISO→pin 2 and CS→pin 1.
      3. 00 00 00: the chip isn't powered or GND is missing, or MOSI/MISO are swapped. Check VCC→pin 8, GND→pin 4, and MOSI→pin 5.
      4. Odd values like 7F BF 0A, or a changing ID: a loose wire or a clock problem. Shorten the wires and re-solder CLK→pin 6.
      5. Then read with "read twice". Matching reads mean the wiring is solid enough to write.

      Two ways to power the chip

      The NOR needs 3.3 V from exactly one source. Which one decides whether you connect the programmer's VCC wire.

      Recommended Board unplugged, programmer powers the chip

      1. AC unplugged. Connect all six wires, including VCC 3.3 V → pin 8.
      2. The programmer also back-feeds the standby rail the chip shares with the southbridge. If reads are unstable, ground JL5110 (/SSB_RESET) on EDM-010/020, or lift pin 8, or remove the chip.
      3. This is the only method to use on EDM-03x and EDM-04x, where the southbridge reset point isn't mapped.

      Advanced Board powered in standby, programmer doesn't

      1. Connect CS, MISO, MOSI, CLK and GND only. Do not connect the programmer's 3.3 V wire: the console's standby rail already powers the chip, and two supplies on one rail can damage the programmer, the chip or the board.
      2. In standby the southbridge is awake and also drives these same SPI lines. Unless it's held in reset, reads come back garbled and a write can corrupt the NOR. On EDM-010/020, hold JL5110 (/SSB_RESET) to GND for the whole read/write.
      3. Plug AC in only after the wires (and the reset jumper) are in place, keep the console off (standby, not powered on), and unplug AC before removing anything.
      4. Not for EDM-03x/04x until a reset point is confirmed. Use the unplugged method there.

      Dump / flash on EDM-04x

      1. Same 3.3 V chip as the launch boards: a 3.3 V-modded CH341A, no 1.8 V adapter.
      2. AC unplugged. Clip on with the red wire on pin 1 (the dot). Programmer tab → Connect → expect EF 40 15, "Winbond W25Q16JV".

      4. Save the backup before editing. Disc ↔ Digital switching is disabled for Slim dumps; serial/SKU edits and the health check work.

      Finding the points yourself

      1. NOR: confirmed on EDM-041 as a 3.3 V Winbond W25Q16JV next to the southbridge (see above). On other revisions, read the marking: 25Q16JV/W25Q…V means 3.3 V; 25U…/W25Q…W/GD25LQ means 1.8 V (add a 1.8 V adapter). The Programmer tab also flags the voltage from the chip ID.
      2. UART: look for a cluster of small round test pads near the southbridge on the underside. In standby (AC in, console off), TX idles ≈3.3 V. RX is usually also pulled up. Check GND with continuity.
      3. Connect TX→RX and RX→TX with the adapter at 3.3 V. If the line voltage drops sharply when you connect, stop and recheck the adapter and pads.
      4. The UART pad map is above. Other community maps exist on repair forums (e.g. TronicsFix "EDM-041 UART hook up"). Always confirm with a meter.
      5. If you get an EDM-04x board service manual or boardview, send it over and the exact points can be added here.

      3 beeps as soon as it's plugged in

      The southbridge checks the NOR before anything else powers up. When it doesn't like what it finds, it beeps three times and stops. Work through these in order.

      1. Connect UART and plug in. If $$ [MANU] UART CMD READY appears, the southbridge is running and reading the NOR, so the problem is the NOR's contents. No banner at all points to a missing or dead NOR, or the southbridge.
      2. Read the codes, then clear the log. Press Clear log, unplug for 10 seconds and plug back in without pressing power. A full log can cause the beeps on its own.
      3. Written a donor dump? Those codes aren't yours. The error log lives on the NOR, so the donor's history comes with it. The UART tab tags those entries In loaded dump when the same dump is open in the NOR Editor.
      4. Check the dump in the NOR Editor. The health check verifies the identity block checksum (0x1C4000–0x1C41FF). If it fails, the console beeps 3 times. When a single corrupted byte is to blame, as on a backup read from a failing chip, one click fixes it.
      5. Rebuild with Restore identity, not by editing fields one at a time. The LAN MAC sits inside the checksummed block, and changing it by hand breaks the checksum. Also make sure the donor is set for the same HDMI chip as your board; NOR Studio warns you if it isn't.
      6. Read the chip back while it's on the board and use Compare against the file you wrote. A perfect match rules out the soldering and the write.
      7. Beeps only when you press power? That's a different fault. Check the power/eject button board's flex cable first.

      Fixed: EDM-041 Slim

      The case this troubleshooter came from.

      • ✕
        3 beeps on plug-in, UART banner present, no new codesThe NOR chip had died. A new chip with the console's own backup still beeped, and so did a donor dump with the console's identity copied in.
      • !
        The donor dump unmodified bootedThat proved the board was fine. The codes on UART had all come with the donor dump, not from this console.
      • ✓
        One corrupted byte in the backupThe console's own backup had 0x00 at 0x1C4000 instead of 0x30, read from the dying chip. That broke the identity block checksum. Donor dump + own identity + 0x30 booted on the console's own serial and MACs.

      Finding a donor dump

      NOR Studio doesn't host dumps. Every dump contains Sony's firmware and another console's serial, MACs and keys. Repair forums share them in their firmware threads, for example BadCaps and TronicsFix.

      • ✓
        Same board familySlim for Slim (EDM-04x), launch for launch (EDM-010/020), EDM-03x for EDM-03x. Same edition is easiest.
      • ✓
        Clean health checkOpen it in the NOR Editor. No failed checks, including the identity block checksum.
      • ✓
        Same HDMI chip as your boardConsole information shows which HDMI chip a dump is set for: Realtek RTD2175P or Panasonic MN864739. It must match the chip on your board. Restore identity warns you if it doesn't.
      • ✓
        Look at its error logThe health check reads the donor's error log. APU, power or thermal faults mean that console was sick. The data may still be fine, but a clean log is a safer bet.
      • ✓
        Keep an untouched copyIf a rebuild beeps, writing the donor as-is tells you whether the board or the rebuild is the problem.

      Put your own identity back

      A donor dump written as-is makes your console that other console: same serial, same MACs. Anything tied to that serial, like a PSN ban, applies to both, and two consoles online with one identity cause trouble.

      1. Open the donor dump in the NOR Editor.
      2. Press Restore identity and pick your console's own backup.
      3. Apply, check the byte diff, save and write.
      4. No backup of your own? The serial and model are on the console's label. The MACs and console keys can't be recovered without a dump.

      Converting the NOR for an HDMI chip swap

      Slim boards ship with one of two HDMI chips, and the NOR says which one the console should expect (0x1C4062: 01 Realtek RTD2175P, FF Panasonic MN864739). Converter boards such as BGA Tech's Realtek → Panasonic mod replace a dead Realtek chip with a Panasonic one, so the NOR has to be switched to Panasonic too.

      BGA Tech's method is a base file: a known-good Panasonic NOR for the board family, with the console's serial numbers copied in. Restore identity in NOR Studio does the same job and copies the MAC addresses, model and Kiban ID as well.

      1. Read and save the console's original NOR before touching anything. It's the only copy of its identity.
      2. Get the Panasonic base file for your board family from the converter's maker. BGA Tech provides theirs for its converter (see its GitHub).
      3. Open the base file in the NOR Editor. Console information should say HDMI chip: Panasonic MN864739, and the health check should pass.
      4. Press Restore identity and pick the console's original dump. Keep everything ticked. The "Different HDMI chip" note is expected here: the base file's Panasonic setting stays.
      5. Save, then write and verify with the Programmer tab (or your T76).
      6. Fit the converter board, connect UART, plug in and read the codes. Clear the log first: it came with the base file.

      Things to know

      • ✓
        Copy the whole identity, not just the serialThe video method copies the serial and motherboard serial only, so the console keeps the base file's MAC addresses. Every console converted from the same base file then shares those MACs, which causes conflicts on the same network. Restore identity copies your own.
      • !
        Firmware version comes from the base fileConsole information shows the base file's firmware version, which may be older than what's on the console's SSD. Update the system software once it boots.
      • !
        The model number comes from the base file unless you restore itRestore identity sets your own model (SKU) and region back. Keep that ticked.
      • ✓
        Checksum is handledRestore identity keeps the identity block checksum correct. The health check confirms it before you write.
      • ✓

      Getting started

      Browser

      UART uses the Web Serial API, available in desktop Chrome, Edge, Opera and other Chromium browsers. Firefox and Safari can still use the NOR editor and database. Save this page and open it straight from disk — no install, no admin rights, works offline once the database is downloaded.

      ⚡ CH341A voltage (read this first)

      Most of the common black CH341A boards put about 5 V on their signal pins even with the 3.3 V supply selected. That can damage the NOR chip or the PS5's southbridge. Before using a CH341A on a PS5, either as an SPI programmer or as a UART adapter, it needs the 3.3 V mod. Check yours with a multimeter: plug it into USB with nothing else attached and measure from GND to CS/MOSI (or TX in serial mode). About 3.3 V is safe, about 5 V is not. 1.8 V NOR chips (MX25U…, W25Q…W, GD25LQ…) additionally need a 1.8 V adapter.

      UART and the programmer do different jobs. UART (the pads near the southbridge) reads and clears error codes; it cannot read or write the NOR. Dumping/flashing needs the programmer clipped onto the NOR chip itself. On CH341A boards that have a mode jumper, it switches the board between programmer mode and serial (UART) mode.

      CH341A programmer setup

      Windows: browsers can only talk to the CH341A through the generic WinUSB driver. Do this once: download Zadig (zadig.akeo.ie), plug in the CH341A, choose Options → List All Devices, select the CH341A entry (USB ID 1A86 5512, often shown as "USB-EPP/I2C… CH341A"), pick WinUSB and click Replace Driver. Replug it. Note: NeoProgrammer needs the original CH341PAR driver — to go back, uninstall the device in Device Manager (tick "delete the driver") and reinstall CH341PAR.

      Linux: add SUBSYSTEM=="usb", ATTR{idVendor}=="1a86", ATTR{idProduct}=="5512", MODE="0666" to /etc/udev/rules.d/99-ch341a.rules, then sudo udevadm control --reload and replug. macOS: works as-is.

      Workflow: Programmer tab → Connect → check the detected chip and voltage → Read chip (it lands in the NOR Editor) → Save backup → edit in the NOR Editor → back to Programmer → Write to chip. Write asks how to proceed: Read first (recommended) saves a backup and rewrites only the changed 4 KB sectors; Write without reading erases and rewrites the whole chip with no backup, after a warning. Both verify afterwards.

      Other programmers: XGecu T76 — read/write with Xgpro and open the .bin here for editing. The FT232R is a UART adapter (set its jumper to 3.3 V) — use it in the UART tab.

      UART adapter driver

      If your adapter doesn't appear in the port picker, install its USB-serial driver (CH340/CH341, CP210x or FTDI) and replug it.

      Why did the old app say "database download error"?

      PS5 NOR Modifier fetched its database from http://uartcodes.com/xml.php. That plain-HTTP endpoint no longer serves the database reliably, and the app swallowed the real error. NOR Studio pulls from a maintained HTTPS source, caches the result in your browser, and lets you import a file if you're offline.

      UART protocol

      Commands are sent as command:XX, where XX is the sum of the command's character codes, masked to one byte, in hex. For example errlog 0 becomes errlog 0:BA. Replies start with OK or NG; the third field of an OK reply is the error code.

      NOR edits

      Edition, serial and SKU live in the NVS area (0x1C4000–0x1D0000). NOR Studio only patches inside that area, shows every byte it will change, and never touches your original file. Serial, motherboard serial, WiFi and LAN MAC are paired with the console's hardware — keep the matching set together.
